Key takeaway

How to read this page. Each signal is scored against its own history, from −3 to +3. A score near 0 is normal. Below −1.5 is unusually low; above +1.5 is unusually high. The last column says what the reading means for a portfolio manager.

About the data

  • Long-term vs short-term. Long-term signals show what markets pay investors to hold risk over years. Short-term signals can change quickly and do not predict near-term direction on their own.
  • Changes. 3-month and 1-year columns show how much the reading moved, in percentage points (pp) for rates and spreads.
  • Review. "Pending review" means Team 4 has not yet checked the number. Numbers may be updated after review.
  • VIX row. The reading of 14.28 and score of −1.63 come from the strategy memo of Sep 22, 2026. The memo measures VIX against its downside variation only; the usual score would be −0.66.
  • Data format. Following Team 1's conventions, rates are stored as decimals (0.025 means 2.5%). Every row carries a data_status (real, synthetic or placeholder) and a release_id. Only real rows are read as signals.
